Research Theme

Chester's research is situated in the Extended Reality (XR) area with spatial computing, focusing more specifically on passthrough Mixed / Augmented Reality (MR / AR) devices, and uses Computer Vision (CV), multimodal AI, and spatial Computer Graphics (CG) to envision new Human-Computer Interactions. This theme unfolds as follows:



XR Terminology Concepts: Proposing new concepts that expand how XR is defined and articulate new immersive experiences, with potential knowledge and inspiration drawn from adjacent fields.

MR Head-Mounted Display Systems: Building on commodity standalone MR devices, leveraging their specialized sensing and rendering capacities as novel Input1 / Output2 channels for envisioning ubiquitously situated interactions in the future.

MR with Robotics and Controlled Mechatronics: Coupling MR with concurrent and companion mechatronics and robotics, extending how users perceive, comprehend, and intervene in the physical world through deeper and materialized interactions.


1 human user inputs: eye tracking, hand tracking, etc.; environmental inputs: scene modeling and reconstruction, multimodal labeled segmentation & object tracking, etc.
2 spatial manifestations of graphical treatments in different combinations of passthrough and virtuality, e.g., spatially anchored simulations and diminished synthesis for hiding elements





Special Vision - Temporality

Spatial computing emerged from the desire to move past WIMP3 and embed computing back into space, the fundamental dimension of human existence. Beyond the explicit spatiality4, human life also unfolds — although more implicitly — continuously over time, and is thereby equally bound to temporality5. Chester's vision leverages the established capabilities of spatial computing and pivots them toward temporality as an equal dimension of experience.

5 practical and theoretical attributes of time, perception of temporal progression (flow or shifting), active navigation capability & passive indication, adjunct attributes (e.g., causality), etc.





Research Roadmap

Theme & Vision

rulAR

rulAR leverages real-time scene reconstruction resources and optimization algorithms to intelligently label measurement-critical spatial points for users.

The raw scene plane meshes are synthesized into approximated complete rectangles using a two-step optimization process. Based on the processed scene, immersive visualization and intelligent labeling are delivered.

Spatiality Decoder

Spatiality Decoder enables real-time capturing using hand gestures, hides the computational synthesis process under an animation metaphor, and then delivers an inhabitable scene in situ.

Users naturally capture photographic images in the scene using a finger-frame gesture, and the system automatically processes and synthesizes them into 3D Gaussian Splats. All the computational processing hides behind a tearing-frame metaphor.

Transcendent Reality

Transcendent Reality proposes the concept of constructing spacetime as one unified substrate for virtual-real coupling expression, delivering spatial manifestation over the temporal continuum.

The system, Reality Transcender, demonstrates experiences of the concept across natural and artificial domains in three daily scenarios.

Two scenarios, solar & shadow and popularity, are demonstrated here. The user "travels across time" to experience realities that transcend the actual present. Manifestations are perceived with as little effort as content occurring in actuality, preserving full information modality.

Transcendent Reality embodies the notion of the temporal continuum: the time aspect within space-time continuum theories, which is as real and as uniform as space.

The schematic narrates the structure of realities unfolding around the concept: space and time form a unified substrate, and content happens inside it. Such uniform and non-directional modeling of time diminishes (but does not negate) empirical temporal attributes (e.g., causality and the flow of time), envisioning interface-enabled capabilities as super-empirical experiences.

Beyond Motion Intent

The system provides visualization elements for daily-encountered mobile robots, including behavioral intents and decision-making-dependent spatial mapping.

Visualization designs include forward pathing, a social cost zone, and a safety-stop zone. Users observe pattern changes according to changes in the proxemic situation. Designs are created and evaluated to provide spontaneous comprehension without the need for explanation.

The mobile robot is implemented with a real navigation algorithm, simulating readily deployable human-robot interaction situations.

The simulated robot is controlled through ROS2 and deployed in a reconstructed Unity scene, behaving as if it were actually deployed in the hallway.

The system aims to address short-term future decision-making for users with proxemic transparency in encounter scenarios.
